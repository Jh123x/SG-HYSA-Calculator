# Understanding EIR: How Effective Interest Rates Work for Singapore Savings Accounts

When comparing savings accounts in Singapore, you'll often see phrases like "up to 3.35% p.a." But the interest you actually earn — your Effective Interest Rate or EIR — depends on several factors. Understanding EIR is the key to choosing the right account and maximising your returns.

## EIR vs Nominal Interest Rate

The **nominal interest rate** is the advertised rate before compounding and bonus conditions. For example, OCBC might advertise a base rate of 0.05% plus bonus interest of up to 3.30% from different categories.

The **Effective Interest Rate (EIR)** is the actual annual percentage you earn on your total deposit, accounting for compounding and which bonus tiers you qualify for. This is the number that matters for your wallet.

## How Bonus Categories Stack

Singapore HYSAs use a category-based bonus system. Here's how it typically works:

- **Base rate:** A small rate (e.g., 0.05%–0.10%) that everyone earns on their entire balance.
- **Salary credit bonus:** Earn an additional bonus (e.g., +0.60%) when you credit your salary to the account.
- **Card spend bonus:** Earn more (e.g., +0.60%) when you spend S$500+ on the bank's card.
- **Save/Grow bonus:** Some banks reward you for increasing your balance month-over-month.
- **Insurance/Investment bonus:** Additional bonus for products like insurance policies or unit trusts.

Your EIR is the **sum of all bonus rates you qualify for**. If you qualify for 3 out of 5 categories, your EIR is the sum of those three bonus rates plus the base rate.

## Why EIR Matters More Than the Advertised Rate

The "up to X%" you see in advertising is the maximum possible EIR — achievable only if you meet every single bonus condition. If you can't credit your salary, or prefer not to use the bank's credit card, your actual EIR will be lower.

For example, a bank advertising 3.35% may actually give you only 0.65% if you only qualify for the base and salary tiers. That's a massive difference.

## Compounding Matters Too

Most Singapore HYSA interest is calculated daily and credited monthly. This means your interest earns interest in subsequent months — compounding works in your favour. The difference between monthly compounding vs annual crediting can add up over time, especially with larger balances.
